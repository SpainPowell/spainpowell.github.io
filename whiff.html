01 · Problem

The group lasts.
The tooling doesn’t.

Private sports pools are social products held together by operational tools. The competition may live in a spreadsheet, the conversation in a group text, and the history nowhere at all. Every new season asks the commissioner to rebuild the same community.

Whiff began with a narrower question: what if college-football Pick’em and Survivor could be the entry point to a durable home for the group itself?

02 · Product thesis

One durable community.
Many reasons to return.

The competition gets people in. The accumulated relationships, rituals, rivalries, and history give them a reason to stay.

Pool is the product’s center of gravity: one identity, roster, role system, chat, invite code, history, and trophy cabinet. Competitions repeat inside it by sport, season, and format.

03 · Pivotal decisions

Three choices shaped the product.

Each decision resolves a tension in the product and leaves behind concrete, shipped proof.

01

One user-facing pool, two durable layers

Keep the architecture. Simplify the noun.

Tension
An earlier product model exposed both a permanent group and a season-format pool, duplicating homes, rosters, chats, join codes, and settings.
Choice
Preserve the two-level data model but expose one user-facing Pool containing repeatable Competitions.
Shipped proof
One home, roster, join code, chat, and creation flow across web and mobile—with concurrent competitions and reproducible eligibility underneath.

System underneath

  • TypeScript/Node monorepo
  • Next.js + React web
  • Expo + React Native mobile
  • Fastify · PostgreSQL · Redis · WebSockets
  • Injected clocks + deterministic fixtures
02

Social by default, private by design

Conversation stays open. Picks stay closed.

Tension
Picks create conversation, but exposing another member’s selection before lock breaks trust and competitive integrity.
Choice
Keep activity contextual, enforce reveal-at-lock on the server, and let only a member deliberately publish their own pick sheet.
Shipped proof
A server-owned state transition reveals group picks only after the slate locks; self-sharing remains an explicit member action.

03

Private competition, shareable stories

Turn the outcome into the invitation.

Tension
The product is intentionally private, but group energy and acquisition happen in external group chats and social channels.
Choice
Turn outcomes into native Story, Post, Square, and Link compositions, with static and animated variants.
Shipped proof
A completed share-sheet and card pipeline turns standings into channel-ready assets without making the pool public.

Shipped engineering

  • Static and animated share-card system
  • Real-time updates with reconnect, backfill, resnapshot, and polling fallback

Still being validated

  • Invite acceptance and participation quality across live pools
  • On-time picking after closed slates
  • Week-over-week operation without developer scoring intervention
  • Apple App Store readiness and longer-term user outcomes

The most important design move was separating the product’s mental model from its storage model. Engineering could retain the two layers needed for history and concurrent competitions while the interface returned to one object people already understood: their pool. The next questions are behavioral, not architectural, and require live-pool evidence.
